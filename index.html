Pharmacy Claims Lakehouse

Legacy pipeline → modern lakehouse · Databricks

A rebuild of the kind of SQL Server and Alteryx reporting pipeline many analytics teams still run, as a governed lakehouse on Databricks. A million synthetic pharmacy claims, with the mess real files have, flow from raw files to business-ready tables and a dashboard. One serverless job runs everything in about 9 minutes.

  • Data quality as a gate: 34 checks with severities and tolerances. A critical failure stops the job before bad numbers reach the dashboard
  • Every row accounted for: reconciliation at each layer, from 998,498 rows received to 887,876 final claims, with every reject given a reason
  • Safe to re-run: incremental loading and idempotent writes give identical results on every run, with an audit table
  • Domain logic in the model: product codes standardised (39% arrived non-standard), and contract precedence avoids a 3.4% double count in rebate estimates
  • 54 design decisions documented, each with its reason and the alternative considered

Drug Unit Converter

Rules engine on public regulatory data · Python

Drug claim lines often arrive with a quantity but no reliable unit: is "10" ten vials, ten mL or ten billing units? This tool works it out from public US reference data (FDA, CMS) and gives a confidence level with its reasons. A small, testable product that answers a costly ambiguity, and admits when it can't.

  • About 99.7% accurate on simulated single-package claims when an allowed amount is present (about 70% without); claims it can't resolve are flagged as undecided, not guessed
  • Scores both readings on structure (whole vials, package counts, whole HCPCS units, waste) and on price against ASP and NADAC
  • Handles billing modifiers: combines JW/JZ waste lines and RT/LT laterality lines, and links KP/KQ lines before converting
  • Single line or CSV batch, with downloadable results. Refuses uploads with patient-like columns
